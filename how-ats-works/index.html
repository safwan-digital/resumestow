How ATS works, in plain words

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is the software a company uses to collect job applications. Names you may have seen: Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Taleo, iCIMS, SAP SuccessFactors. When you apply online, your resume goes into this system first — not to a person.

Step 1: The parse

The ATS reads your file and tries to sort its text into boxes: name, email, phone, each job with its title, company and dates, education, and skills. This step is purely mechanical. Anything it cannot read as clean text — scanned images, text inside graphics, two-column layouts, tables — comes out empty, scrambled, or filed in the wrong box.

Step 2: The search

Recruiters then search the database the way you search Google: with keywords from the job description. "SEO", "Google Ads", "project management", "Python". If your resume never uses the exact words the job asks for, you do not appear in the results — even if you did the same work under a different name.

Step 3: The knock-out

Many applications add screening questions and simple filters: years of experience calculated from your dates, location, work authorisation, required certifications. Missing or unparsable dates can make five years of experience look like zero.

What this means for you

You cannot control a company's system, but you fully control the three things it depends on: a file it can read (single column, real text, standard headings), the job's own keywords used honestly where they describe your real experience, and complete contact and date information. Common myths

"A beautiful designed resume stands out." To a human, maybe. To the parser that reads it first, design is usually the reason it fails. Send the designed version only when a human asks for it directly.

"I should copy the whole job description into my resume in white text." Recruiters and modern systems spot this, and it is dishonest. Use the keywords that genuinely match your experience, in real sentences.

"One resume fits every job." Keyword matching is per job description. Serious applicants adjust their resume's wording for each serious application — it takes ten minutes and it is the highest-value step in the process.
